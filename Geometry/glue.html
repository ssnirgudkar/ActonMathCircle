Glue — How high can you get?

Place squares on a grid. Each square's label must equal the sum of all the squares it touches along an edge. Start with two 1s — then try to go as high as you can.

← All Explorations · Problem · Arithmetic & Geometry

The Rules

  1. Start by placing two 1×1 squares labeled 1 anywhere on the grid.
  2. Next, glue a square labeled 2, then 3, then 4, and so on — always the next integer.
  3. A square's label must equal the sum of labels of all squares sharing an edge with it.
  4. A square's edge length may not exceed its label. (It may be smaller.)
  5. Squares must align to the grid and may not overlap.

Things to Discover

① How high can you get?

Work with your partner to place squares as high as you can. When you get stuck, hit Reset and try a different arrangement from the start. What's your group's record?

② Does size matter?

You can place a square with label 5 as a 1×1, 2×2, 3×3, 4×4, or 5×5. Try each. When does a bigger square help you go higher? When does it hurt?

③ Change the starting n

Try starting with n=3 (three 1s, then jump to 3, 4, 5...). Does changing n make it easier or harder? Which n gives the best results?

④ The sum rule — why?

Pick any square you've placed. Check that its label really does equal the sum of all its edge-touching neighbors. Now ask: could you ever have a square whose label is larger than the sum of all squares placed so far? Why or why not?
